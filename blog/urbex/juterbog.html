Jüterbog

August 16, 2025 · Jüterbog, Brandenburg, Germany

Approximate area only. Exact locations aren't shared.

This one's a little sneaky, historically. Germany wasn't allowed to have an air force after WWI, so in 1933 they built this place in secret and called it a “technical school” for a civilian pilot academy. In reality it was training aircraft engineers and mechanics for the Luftwaffe, about 1,500 in the first year alone. Even the main buildings are laid out in the shape of an airplane.

After 1945 the Soviets took over and used it to train tank and artillery officers, while MiG-21s and later MiG-23s flew out of the airfield next door until 1992. They left a lot behind: Guards badges tiled into the walls, Soviet newspapers still pasted to the plaster, and walls of magazine cutouts from someone's bunk.

The best part was the old sports hall, with its huge yellow windows and a round porthole window you can stand in.

It's been empty since the Soviets left, slowly turning back into forest. Well, mostly empty.

We got a little too comfortable. We hadn't seen a single sign of security or cameras the whole time, so we were wandering around shooting photos right out in the open. Then we saw a truck.

We ran and hid around a corner, staring in the direction we were sure he'd come from. He came from behind us. “Entschuldigung.”

Luckily I speak enough German to actually have a conversation. I told him we were just doing photography, we hadn't broken anything, we'd been really respectful of the history of the place, we meant no harm, and we could leave right now. He walked us back to our car and asked us, politely, not to come back.

Second time a German security guard has said that to me. I'm starting to sense a pattern.

Plus a couple on film.
