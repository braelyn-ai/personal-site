The Seismic Office, Treasure Island

May 11, 2025 demolished · Treasure Island, San Francisco, California

Approximate area only. Exact locations aren't shared.

Treasure Island is a fake island in the middle of the bay, built for the 1939 World's Fair and then taken over by the Navy, who ran it as a base until 1997. Since then it's been slowly redeveloped, and a lot of the old Navy buildings have just been sitting there empty.

This one turned out to be a seismic retrofitting office, full of plans for the city's buildings. I found it on Google Maps while hunting for spots, and one day we were riding by and I thought, let's stop and take a look.

Getting in

It was very securely boarded up. Clearly people had been sneaking in, and someone did not want that. The only way I could find was to climb onto a balcony, then do a pull-up, three stories up, to get through a fourth-story window. My riding partner was not about to climb that, so she waited outside.

That first time was the most pristine I ever saw it, though there was still plenty of graffiti. There were also people living in there, so I gave them their space.

The next time, I went to where they'd been living, and the art was incredible: graffiti, but also written and drawn pieces. There were needles everywhere. And then I found the room: endless drawers of schematics and plans for almost every city-owned building in San Francisco, going back 50+ years.

The door to that room said “Danger: Asbestos, Do Not Enter,” but there was no sign of any actual asbestos, or of anyone ever removing it. I'm pretty sure it was just there to keep people out of the plans in case anyone wanted them later. Somebody had spray-painted a terrifying monster on the other side of that door, so walking in scared the hell out of you.

The fire

When I came back again, the room with all the schematics had burned to the ground, and it took about a quarter of the building with it. Decades of plans, just gone. Doors had been busted open, it wasn't hard to get in anymore, and there were kids hanging out inside. It had clearly turned into more of a bando than a piece of preserved history.

The roof at sunset was still unreal though, with the whole San Francisco skyline and the Bay Bridge across the water.

Gone

So it wasn't a surprise when I went back the last time and the entire thing had been demolished. Not even a clue that it was ever there. Just a fence, a dirt lot, and a “Danger: Construction Area” sign.

That's the thing about urbex. These places don't wait for you.
