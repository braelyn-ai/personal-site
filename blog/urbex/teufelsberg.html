Teufelsberg

August 18, 2025 commercialized · Grunewald, Berlin, Germany

Approximate area only. Exact locations aren't shared.

Fair warning: this one isn't proper urban exploration, and I knew that going in. Teufelsberg is a museum now. It's been commercialized. But it's one of the most historic places in Berlin, it's still abandoned military infrastructure, and I just had to go see it.

Teufelsberg literally means “Devil's Mountain,” and the whole hill is fake. After WWII, Berlin was rubble, so from 1950 to 1972 they piled about 26 million cubic meters of it here. Underneath all that is an unfinished Nazi military college designed under Albert Speer. The Allies tried to blow it up, failed, and just buried it instead.

Then the Americans and the British put a spy station on top. From the early '60s until 1990, the NSA and GCHQ listened in on radio and phone traffic across East Germany and beyond from inside those giant white “golf balls.” Up to around 1,200 Americans and a few hundred Brits worked up here in shifts.

It closed in 1992, and after that everyone had a plan for it: a hotel, apartments, a spy museum, even a “Vedic University of Peace” from David Lynch. None of it happened. In the meantime it became an incredibly powerful place for artists to express themselves and protest: the division of the city, the war, and the cultural fallout that came with it.

Then it was bought, and now you pay to get in. Those artists painted all over these walls in protest, not so someone could profit off it, so it feels a little weird. It's a protected monument now and a massive open-air gallery, and the view of Berlin at sunset from the top of the radome tower is unreal. The film shots are my favorites from this one.

Is it worth seeing? Yes. It's a pretty hike up to the top, and it's a real landmark. But I honestly struggle to include it on this list, because I don't consider it true urban exploration.
