Indiana Women's Prison

June 29, 2022 · Near Eastside, Indianapolis, Indiana

Approximate area only. Exact locations aren't shared.

This place has history. In October 1873, seventeen women moved into a brick building on Randolph Street, right across the street from the fancy Victorian mansions of Woodruff Place. It was the first prison in the United States built just for women. It stayed here for 136 years, until the women's prison moved to the west side in 2009. After that it became the Indianapolis Re-Entry Educational Facility, a minimum-security program for men getting ready to go home, until it closed for good in 2017.

Since then the whole 15-acre campus has sat fenced off. Demolition started in 2018, and a bunch of the buildings were already gone or going when I went. The city took it over in a land swap in 2024 and is still figuring out what to do with it.

Scouting

I scouted it from outside the fence first. There were already piles of rubble where buildings used to be.

Going in

A week later we went in. Had to peep it before it was all demolished.

Because it was actively being demolished, every door was unlocked and just left open. I saw my opportunity and I couldn't resist. Somebody had already cut a hole in the fence, so I figured why not, and walked in with a friend.

We came out between buildings right as the sun was setting over the campus. The old brick buildings look a lot more like a college than a prison from out here. Then you walk into the infirmary and there's still an X-ray machine bolted to the wall.

After dark it got a lot creepier: the kitchen, offices with the Department of Correction seal still painted on the wall, and the tunnels and boiler rooms underneath it all, by flashlight.

All of the buildings were connected underneath by tunnels, which was super interesting. A lot of them were just utility tunnels, but we climbed through them anyway. Plenty of them had asbestos that was being removed before demolition, which is what the little clean room was for: somewhere for the crews to clean themselves off after working on it.

While we were down in the tunnels, a scary man with a hacksaw came walking through. We said hi. He just grunted and walked on by, dragging the hacksaw against the wall. We were terrified for our lives. Turns out he was just there for the copper.

To end the night, we climbed the ladder up to the gymnasium roof with a couple of beers and had a sip overlooking the skyline on a warm summer night. It was pretty nice.
