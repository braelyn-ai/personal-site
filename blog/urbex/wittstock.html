Wittstock

June 24, 2026 · Wittstock, Brandenburg, Germany

Approximate area only. Exact locations aren't shared.

Wittstock is kind of an urbex paradise. This tiny town in northern Brandenburg spent almost fifty years as a Soviet garrison town, and when the Soviets finally packed up in 1994 they left a LOT behind. Add a dead textile industry on top of that and you've got more abandoned spots than you could hit in a week. We had one afternoon, so we barely scratched the surface.

I went with my partner Ellie and Sophie, my best friend at the time.

I found Wittstock on an urban exploration website, and there were a couple of spots near each other that I figured we could pack into one trip. The most exciting was the old Red Army base where the 33rd Fighter Regiment was stationed.

The Soviet side

The Red Army rolled into Wittstock on May 2, 1945 and didn't leave until June 1994. Just outside town was Alt Daber airfield, home to the 33rd Fighter Regiment and their MiG-29s, and south of town was the Bombodrom, a massive bombing and training range the Soviets used until 1993. When the Bundeswehr tried to keep bombing it after reunification, locals fought them for almost twenty years and won. Iconic.

A lot of the runway and open space has since been turned into a solar farm, which is great progress. I'm also thankful they left so many of the old military buildings to rot, because it meant I got to explore them.

The place we got into still has Soviet fingerprints everywhere. There's a theater with the stage still standing, a gym with “1UP” sprayed over the basketball hoop, and a Russian slogan tiled into a wall that translates to roughly: “Competent operation and timely charging of batteries is your contribution to combat readiness.” Truly inspiring stuff.

And then there's Lenin. He's still out there mid-speech, except someone smashed his face off. Someone else left a sign at his feet, in German: “I, called Lenin, have lost my face, but didn't we do you a huge favor in 1991? Think about it!”

So what was the favor? Probably leaving. In 1990 Moscow let East Germany go without firing a shot, and in March 1991 the Soviet Union ratified the treaty that made reunification official and promised to pull every last soldier out of Germany. Then in December 1991 the Soviet Union just… stopped existing. That's about 340,000 troops packing up and going home, the last of them in 1994, which is exactly how places like this ended up empty. So Lenin's not wrong. Without that favor there'd be no abandoned garrisons, and I'd have nothing to explore. Thanks, I guess?

The cloth factory

The cloth factory was a fun bonus. We were driving through town on the way out, saw a massive abandoned factory, and thought, “Huh, well, we should go take a look at that.” So we went in and explored. There wasn't a ton to it, but it was a nice find.

It turns out it's the old Tuchfabrik, a cloth factory that's been there since 1905. The six-story brick building went up in 1917, and the place made uniforms through both World Wars. One of its co-owners starting in 1911 was Günther Quandt, as in the Quandt family that later ended up owning a huge chunk of BMW. After the war the Soviets dismantled the machines and shipped them east, the GDR ran it as VEB Tuchfabrik Wittstock, and it finally shut down for good in 1997.

Now it's huge empty halls, rusted pipes, ivy everywhere, and windows looking out over the rooftops of the old town. It's one of those places where nature is very obviously winning.

And a few on film.

There's so much more in Wittstock that we didn't get to. I'll be back.
