Nazi bunkers in the Calanques

October 7, 2022 · Les Goudes, Marseille, France

Approximate area only. Exact locations aren't shared.

The Calanques are the dramatic limestone cliffs and inlets just south of Marseille, and at the very tip, around the little fishing village of Les Goudes, they're covered in WWII bunkers. In 1943 the Germans extended the Atlantic Wall along the Mediterranean (they called it the Südwall), pouring concrete blockhouses, gun pits and galleries all along this coast to stop an Allied landing. The landing came anyway in August 1944, and Marseille was liberated by the end of the month.

I did all three spots in one afternoon hike out of Les Goudes, on my way through Marseille.

The Maronaise bunkers

First up, a cluster of German blockhouses on the rocks above the Anse de la Maronaise. Every inch is covered in graffiti now, and one of them has a long observation room with a slit looking straight out to sea, built to watch for ships that came a year later.

Batterie de Croisette

Next, an old French stone fort out on Cap Croisette, with an arched gate and a lookout tower. It was a French coastal battery long before the war, and the Germans took it over and fortified it for the Südwall. There's even a German archive photo of them test-firing guns here in 1943. Underneath there are tunnels you can crawl through.

Batterie Napoléon

Last, the gun positions on the ridge above Les Goudes, looking out at Île Maire. The round gun pits still have their mounting rings in the ground. There's been a battery up here since the 1690s, rebuilt under Napoleon in 1811. On August 28, 1944, Moroccan goumiers fought the last coastal battle of the Provence campaign at the fort here, the same day Marseille's German commander surrendered.

Then I caught the sunset over the islands, which honestly was the perfect way to end a day of climbing through Nazi concrete.
