Maharashtra and Goa on Scram 411s

December 11, 2024 · Royal Enfield Scram 411

Why this ride?

Natalia was my best friend at the time. We did almost everything together, and she'd recently gotten into motorcycles and was loving the freedom that came with it.

She got invited to a wedding in Mumbai, and we decided we should go a week early and ride motorcycles around the country. A lot of people told us this was a terrible idea and we should not do it. And we especially shouldn't do it without a tour company guiding us. Which, being as stubborn as we both are, meant we obviously had to do it alone.

This was the first big international motorcycle trip I'd ever done, and I was a little bit terrified. I'm incredibly thankful I did it, though, because now I know what I'm capable of, and it unlocked every other trip on this blog.

Day 1: Mumbai

When we got to Mumbai, we couldn't find any way to book motorcycles in advance. It was nearly impossible, so we figured if we turned up with enough money, we could probably just find some. We hunted around a couple of motorcycle shops before we finally found someone willing to rent to us for the week. They were Scram 411s, not the Himalayan 450s we'd hoped for, but they did the job.

We'd haphazardly ratchet-strapped our bags on, which in retrospect was a terrible way to carry everything we owned. It even ended with my exhaust burning a hole through one of the backpacks, which set my GoPro on fire, and I lost all the footage I'd taken.

Our first day in Mumbai was confusing as hell and a little terrifying. We had no idea how to navigate the roads yet. Riding in India is an art, not a science. You just have to feel the road and the way people move, and if you hesitate, people will take advantage of it and cut in front of you.

One of the biggest challenges for me was people seeing my hair or my skin and swarming us, wanting to take pictures or touch me. It was incredibly uncomfortable, and it made it that much harder to know who I could trust on this trip.

Day 2: Varandha Ghat, Mahabaleshwar, Pratapgad and Panhala

We had a couple of places we wanted to see, and built the route around them. The first stop was in the mountains: Varandha Ghat, a really beautiful road that people online had recommended to us.

On the other side, back around through the mountains again, was Pratapgad Fort. Both were really beautiful. We saw cute monkeys and a lot of gorgeous mountain views.

But every view was swallowed by the smog and pollution in the air. You couldn't see far at all before the smog took over. It makes for a dramatic photo, but it was terrible to breathe. The air always smelled like burnt rubber. I never got a real breath of fresh air the whole trip.

We always took the smaller roads when we could. Natalia wasn't a huge fan of off-road, so we didn't do a lot of it, just some when we had to. We passed a ton of abandoned infrastructure I really wanted to go explore, but again, my riding partner was not as eager.

Day 3: Kolhapur down to Goa

Day 3 was the hardest day for me. I was terribly thirsty, and we'd been told there were only two brands of bottled water we could drink if we didn't want to get sick. We ran out, and went village to village looking for one of those two brands. Eventually I broke down. I needed to get the smog out of my mouth, and I was at risk of passing out, so I drank a different brand.

That was a terrible mistake. I got incredibly sick that night and spent the whole night awake, suffering. By morning I couldn't stand the thought of anything with Indian spices in it, and since almost every meal and snack had some kind of spice, pretty much all I could eat for the rest of the trip was bread and watermelon. After that we rigged up a way to carry a few gallons of water each, so we never ran into that problem again.

Goa was our rest day. We spent most of it at the resort, just catching our breath and processing everything we'd seen. A lot of really bad, crazy shit happens in the Indian countryside. I'm very thankful I experienced it, but some of it still sits with me, even years later.

Day 4: Up the coast to Ganpatipule

On the way back up we took the coastline, sticking to the water as much as we could and spending the nights in coastal towns. It was beautiful, but in a very different way than the mountains.

Day 5: The ferry that wasn't

Eventually we made it up to Sakhari to take the ferry, only to find out it wasn't running. We had to turn around and go the long way, which turned a four-hour day into an eight-hour day.

We stopped early at a place called AeroVillage, which has its own runway, and I was so excited to meet some fellow pilots. But the runway was overgrown and there were no planes. A local did take us on a fun jeep ride through the mountainside, though.

Day 6: Back to Mumbai

Day 6 was the last day, a straight shot back to Mumbai. On the way we came across an incredible view of a factory. Riding through that whole town felt like being suffocated. It seemed like the source of the smog for the entire country, though I'm sure it's just one of many. It was really interesting to ride so close and see all that industry in progress, but I can't imagine the life expectancy of the people who live there is very long.

A local also helped us replace the clutch lever on Natalia's bike, which was incredibly kind.

I would never do this ride again, and I don't know if I'll ever go back to India.

I'm thankful I saw the challenges this country faces in person. It gave me a lot of perspective on the world, and on people who are far less privileged than I am. That doesn't mean it's somewhere I want to spend more time.

I left with incredible memories and some real trauma. Both made me grow a lot as a person, and I'm grateful for that. And without this trip, none of the others on this blog would have happened.
